# The registry as its own crate

The registry was a module of the engine and read the engine's whole config. This topic takes it out into a crate that stands without the engine, and says where the line between the two runs.

## What asked for it

Op, on 11 November, about the workspace agenda and where its rows go:

> Agenda rows are deprecated by the way, we are getting a mocksoace on clause-dev just for its registry so we move everything to a registry there too. This is why I want a separate registry crates from mocksoace, so there's potential of integrating them to homma instead directly, and have it interoperate fully like a mocksoace registry, without needing mocksoace installed and running it, homma would adopt the registry part on its end and parity comes from using those crates directly

The intent in that is two things. A tool other than the engine keeps a registry by depending on the registry's own crates, with no engine installed. And the two agree because they run the same code, not because one copies what the other does. The rest is kept for provenance.

## Where the line runs

`mockspace-registry` holds what a registry is: the model of a namespace and a row, loading, the checks a schema cannot make, references and their resolution, the stores on a directory or a git ref, the one writer, the merge of two sides row by row, appending to a row, the generated schemas, and the query a command line asks. It depends on `mockspace-store` and on nothing else of mockspace.

The engine keeps what is its own: the config, the documents references are rendered into, the link a `crates::<name>` reference becomes, the view a lint is handed, and the saved verdicts of a lint run.

## What the registry asks of its project

The registry reads a handful of facts of the project it sits in: the repository root, the mock directory, the docs directory, the declared namespaces and stores, and the roots a citation may name. That question is one trait, and whoever holds the registry answers it. The engine answers it from its config. A tool without the engine answers it with a plain value the crate builds from the `[registry]` and `[ref.roots]` tables of a `mockspace.toml`, passing over every key that is the engine's.

Two answers are the engine's alone and default to none: the link of a crate's document and the directory of a crate. A project with no crates has neither.

The declaration is derived in one place. The engine's config hands its `[registry]` and `[ref.roots]` tables to the crate and takes the namespaces, the roots and the frozen, prose and internal sets back, so the two cannot read one config two ways.

## What is left over

The list of keys a `mockspace.toml` may carry at its root sits with the registry's checks and names keys of the engine. It moves to the engine with the check that reads it, as its own change.
