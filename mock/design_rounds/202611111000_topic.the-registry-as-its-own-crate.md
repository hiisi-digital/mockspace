# The registry as its own crate

The registry was a module of the engine and read the engine's whole config. This topic takes it out into a crate that stands without the engine, and says where the line between the two runs.

## What asked for it

Op, on 11 November, about the workspace agenda and where its rows go:

> Agenda rows are deprecated by the way, we are getting a mocksoace on clause-dev just for its registry so we move everything to a registry there too. This is why I want a separate registry crates from mocksoace, so there's potential of integrating them to homma instead directly, and have it interoperate fully like a mocksoace registry, without needing mocksoace installed and running it, homma would adopt the registry part on its end and parity comes from using those crates directly

The intent in that is two things. A tool other than the engine keeps a registry by depending on the registry's own crates, with no engine installed. And the two agree because they run the same code, not because one copies what the other does. The rest is kept for provenance.

## Where the line runs

`mockspace-registry` holds what a registry is: the model of a namespace and a row, loading, the checks a schema cannot make, references and their resolution, the stores on a directory or a git ref, the one writer, the merge of two sides row by row, appending to a row, the generated schemas, and the query a command line asks. It depends on `mockspace-store`, on the one trait saved verdicts are plugged in by (`mockspace-verdicts`, which has no dependency) and on nothing else of mockspace.

The engine keeps what is its own: the config, the documents references are rendered into, the link a `crates::<name>` reference becomes, the view a lint is handed, and the saved verdicts of a lint run.

## What the registry asks of its project

The registry reads a handful of facts of the project it sits in: the repository root, the mock directory, the docs directory, the declared namespaces and stores, and the roots a citation may name. That question is one trait, and whoever holds the registry answers it. The engine answers it from its config. A tool without the engine answers it with a plain value the crate builds from the `[registry]` and `[ref.roots]` tables of a `mockspace.toml`, passing over every key that is the engine's.

Two answers are the engine's alone and default to none: the link of a crate's document and the directory of a crate. A project with no crates has neither.

The declaration is derived in one place. The engine's config hands its `[registry]` and `[ref.roots]` tables to the crate and takes the namespaces, the roots and the frozen, prose and internal sets back, so the two cannot read one config two ways.

## What a passing registry is

Which checks a registry is held to, in what order, and what a shape nobody could verify counts as, is one call of the crate. The engine and a tool without it both make that call, so the two agree by using the same thing. What stays with the caller is what only the caller knows: which files its run changed, the level its project gives an inert key, where it keeps saved verdicts, and how a finding is printed.

The keys of the config are held by whoever reads them. The crate holds the keys of `[registry]`, of `[ref]` and of what is under them. The root of the config carries the engine's own settings, so the list of what may stand there is the engine's, and a tool without the engine is not told that the engine's keys are unknown.

One finding kind is the engine's alone, a reference left unresolved in a rendered document. A tool that renders none never meets it, so the engine lists it.

## Where a project is

Where the root of a project is and which `mockspace.toml` is read is said in one place, in the crate, and the engine's config asks it. The two answers are held together by a test over the layouts a project comes in: the config in the mock directory, at the root, in both, in neither, each with and without a `.git` above.

## The merge driver

The name git knows the registry's merge driver by, and the attribute line that sends the registry's files to it, are the crate's. Two tools that keep a registry in one clone then register one driver under one name. What the driver runs is the tool's own command, and the engine's stays with the engine.

## Saved verdicts

A check that can replay what it found asks a store of saved verdicts through one trait. The lint rules and the registry both need it and neither may depend on the other, so the trait is a crate of its own with no dependency, and both take it from there.
