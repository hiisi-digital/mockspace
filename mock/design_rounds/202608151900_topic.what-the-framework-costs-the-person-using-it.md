# Topic: what the framework costs the person using it

**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `bench-core/`, `bench-harness/`, `bench-macro/`, `bench-matrix/`, and the four consumer trees
**Source:** two cold derivations and their convergence round, `mock/research/202608151554_the-smallest-mechanism-that-is-correct.md` and `mock/research/202608151700_where-the-bench-framework-fights-you.md`, with fifteen probe directories beside them

The fourth subject of this round, and the first opened by the lead designer rather than by the
investigation: the framework is the most used tool in the suite, and using it should be a breeze
rather than a fight. Two experts derived blind and in parallel, then argued to convergence with the
coordinator moderating.

**Nothing here is settled.** It is what two unratified sources agree on, what they contest, and what
they proved. The provenance ladder applies to all of it.

## What the sitting corrected about this round

**Two items this round's doc changelist assigned to change 1 were already performed by PR #21**,
which has since merged. Found independently by both experts, neither reading the other, and verified
against `origin/dev` and `ca58832` directly rather than on their agreement. The changelist carries the
correction; the wording that claimed the tautology deletion was something "nothing else in flight
performs" was a claim about an open branch asserted without reading it, which is the failure this
round exists to fix, occurring inside the document that diagnoses it.

**Three facts in the dispatching brief were false**, all the coordinator's. The `println!` count was
inflated roughly tenfold because `grep 'println!'` also matches `eprintln!`: it is 11, not 107. The
panic-surface counts included `#[cfg(test)]` bodies. One expert reproduced the `println!` figure
without checking it, in the paragraph whose purpose was checking the brief, and said so rather than
quietly correcting it.

## The convergence, and what it rests on

**Absence and zero are different values in every sample field a verdict reads.** Both experts reached
this independently, on **different formats and different instruments**, which is what makes it the
strongest result the pair produced rather than shared drift. One found it on the worker stdout wire,
positional TSV with an arity guard of nine against a twelve-column writer and `digest` at column
eleven, outside the guard. The other found it on the samples CSV, duplicated verbatim in the cache
codec, where pre-digest files default the column and the code says such files are expected.

The consequence lands on this round directly. **Change 1 promotes the digest comparison to
load-bearing for the first time**, and two damaged arms both read `0` and compare equal, so the check
the round is adding is inert in exactly the case it exists for.

Their answers converged on one shape, stated twice:

> A digest comparison concludes agreement only when every arm supplied a digest. Absence is a taint,
> not agreement.

The verdict is three-valued rather than boolean. Naming differs between the two files
(`NotEstablished` against `NotComparable`); the shape does not, and neither is ratified. It is not a
new concept: it is what this round's tainting already does for a structural drop, applied to the
field change 1 is promoting, and carried in `CellRecord` so a run whose check was inert says so in its
own artifact.

**Ordering, reconciled between them:** the arity guard widens first, because it is the only part
change 1 can create on its own; the record verdict lands with change 1; one codec per format follows.
Fixing one format alone leaves the class half closed in a way that reads as closed.

### And change 1 must name a test it breaks

`bench-harness/src/sample.rs:181` and `:197` assert `digest == 0` for older files. **The defect is
pinned by a passing test that is correct for its own purpose**, backward compatibility. If change 1
does not name `csv_parses_perf_columns_and_is_backward_compatible` as a test it changes, the fix lands
red against a test certifying the defect, and the cheapest exit from a red test is reverting the fix.

This was found by one expert checking its own claim that "zero tests name either format" and
discovering it wrong.

## What the argument settled between them

**The declared analysis roles fail open, and a typo is silent.** One expert measured a one-letter
error in a baseline name flipping every delta from `-74.81%` to `+297.03%`. The other measured a typo
in `floor` rendering a report byte-identical to declaring no floor, `0.50x` where the correct spelling
reads `0.38x`, differing only by one absent paragraph. Of `mode`'s four documented values only one is
observable.

They disagreed about where the check belongs. One proposed adding it to `validate_roles`; the other
argued the arm names are not there, checked, and was right, plus a third fact neither had: **an arm has
three identities**, its directory, its dylib filename, and its exported `bench_name`, only the third is
what every grouping keys on, and nothing checks the three agree. The first withdrew its location and
adopted the other's proposal in full. That is the exchange working and it is closed.

## What an attack established rather than refuted

One expert attacked the other's per-arm build-cost section on three axes. The most useful outcome is
that **one axis failed and handed the claim back stronger**: the attacking probe had used a workspace
invocation shape where the tool uses standalone packages, and re-run in the tool's real shape the
original mechanism held, with two properties its author had never tested (no thrash on a second pass,
order-independence under alternation).

One axis landed. The proposed fix's locus is wrong: the target-directory helper is reached from one
call site on the generated-arm path, the measured cost was on a different path, and **zero of 1088 arms
use the composed form**, so the fix as stated changes a path nobody runs. The same locus defect exists
in a second config key.

One axis converted "unpriced" into a structural number without guessing at seconds. **Not one of the
1088 arms varies the framework's feature axis**; the only partition is the pin, and there are three in
the corpus. So the framework crates and their proc-macro dependencies compile 1088 times where three
to six would do, and that sharing is immune to the feature-unification hazard the earlier probe found,
which bounds only the consumers' own support crates.

**The wall-clock question remains unpriced and both said so.** The current harness cannot host it: it
is a per-call runtime harness with calibration, batches and dlopen, and a build-time comparison has no
per-call quantity. The measurement is specified in the second file, including parallelism as an axis
rather than held at one, because cargo serialises on the package lock and that is where the answer
plausibly flips.

## What was checked and kept

**Keeping something is a result.** One expert's probe exercised `compose_composed_member` over both
member forms, eight fields, five timing knobs and all eight level combinations, and found the surface
correct, which is the replacement its earlier concession was owed.

## The finding that came out of building that probe

**The eight-combination matrix passes on the broken tree.** It varies one knob at a time, so the member
always declares the knob being read and a level that resets the other four is invisible. Only a case
declaring one knob and asserting the others fires. A reviewer looking at a complete-looking matrix
would reasonably call the surface covered.

That case now exists for both member forms on `dev`, each with a negative control that reproduces the
defect's signature.

## Live, and the lead designer's

**What should a non-resolving `floor` or `baseline` do: refuse at load, or run and record that it did
not resolve?** One expert's weight is on refusing, unhedged. What decides it is whether a bench whose
normalisation did not resolve should produce artifacts at all, which is an intent question rather than
a measurement. The three-identities work is a prerequisite for either arm, so it is unblocked
regardless.
