# Topic: what the framework costs the person using it

**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `bench-core/`, `bench-harness/`, `bench-macro/`, `bench-matrix/`, and the four consumer trees
**Source:** two cold derivations and their convergence round, `mock/research/202608151554_the-smallest-mechanism-that-is-correct.md` and `mock/research/202608151700_where-the-bench-framework-fights-you.md`, with sixteen probe directories beside them

The fourth subject of this round, and the first opened by the lead designer rather than by the
investigation: the framework is the most used tool in the suite, and using it should be a breeze
rather than a fight. Two experts derived blind and in parallel, then argued to convergence with the
coordinator moderating.

**Nothing here is settled.** It is what two unratified sources agree on, what they contest, and what
they proved. The provenance ladder applies to all of it.

## The convergence, and the rung it actually earns

**Absence and zero are different values in every sample field a verdict reads.** Both experts reached
this independently on **different formats**, which is what makes it more than shared drift.

- **Samples CSV** (`sample.rs:108-141`, duplicated verbatim at `cache.rs:400-431`): pre-digest files
  default the column, and `sample.rs:138` records that such files are expected.
- **Worker stdout wire**: the guard is `parts.len() >= 9` while the writer emits 12 or 13 columns, and
  **`digest` is column 11, outside the guard**, defaulting to `0`.

**The rung is asymmetric and the weaker half must be stated.** The CSV half is instrument-backed: a
probe exercises the real `load_samples_csv`. The worker-wire half is **reading**-backed for the parser
(its own probe header says it does not test the parser, which is an inline loop with no function
boundary) with an instrument-backed **consequence**: three short lines out of twenty move a mean from
100 ns to 85 ns. The conclusion is not in doubt, since a `>= 9` guard against a 12-column writer is
plain reading anyone can check, but "two instruments" would overstate it by one step.

The consequence lands on this round directly. **Change 1 promotes the digest comparison to
load-bearing for the first time**, and two damaged arms both read `0` and compare equal, so the check
the round is adding is inert in exactly the case it exists for.

Their answers converged on one shape, stated twice:

> A digest comparison concludes agreement only when every arm supplied a digest. Absence is a taint,
> not agreement.

The verdict is three-valued rather than boolean, and carried in `CellRecord` so a run whose check was
inert says so in its own artifact. Naming differs between the two files (`NotEstablished` against
`NotComparable`); the shape does not, and neither is ratified. It is not a new concept: it is what this
round's tainting already does for a structural drop, applied to the field change 1 is promoting.

**Ordering, reconciled between them.** First, widen the arity guard **and** make `digest` parse to a
not-reported value at all three sites; that pair is what closes the manufactured-agreement **hazard**,
and the hazard is the part change 1 creates. The record verdict lands with change 1. One codec per
format follows. Fixing one format alone leaves the class half closed in a way that reads as closed.

## What the argument settled between them

**The declared analysis roles fail open, and a typo is silent.** `with_baseline`
(`analysis.rs:276-281`) is an `if let Some(idx) = ...position(|v| v.name == name)` with no else;
`with_floor` (`analysis.rs:291-296`) carries a doc comment saying outright that a name not among the
variants is ignored. One expert measured a one-letter error in a baseline name flipping a column from
`-74.81%` to `+297.03%`. The other measured a typo in `floor` rendering a report byte-identical to
declaring no floor: `0.50x` where the correct spelling reads `0.38x`. **The distinguishing paragraph
is absent from both wrong reports**, so the typo'd report and the no-floor report differ by nothing;
it is the correct report that carries it.

They disagreed about where the check belongs. One proposed adding it to `validate_roles`; the other
argued the arm names are not there, checked, and was right, plus a third fact neither had: **an arm has
three identities**, its directory, its dylib filename, and its exported `bench_name`, only the third is
what every grouping keys on, and nothing checks the three agree. The first withdrew its location and
adopted the other's proposal in full. That is the exchange working and it is closed.

The prerequisite for either arm of the live question below is the **preflight resolution of
`bench_name`**; the three-identities check is an extension of it, not the prerequisite itself.

## What an attack established rather than refuted

One expert attacked the other's per-arm build-cost section on three axes.

**One axis failed and handed the claim back stronger.** The attacking probe used a workspace
invocation shape where the tool uses standalone packages with `--manifest-path`. Re-run in the tool's
real shape, the original mechanism held, with two properties its author had never tested: no thrash on
a second pass, and order-independence under alternation.

**One axis landed.** The proposed fix's locus is wrong: `arm_target_dir` is reached from one call site
on the generated-arm path (`src/bench.rs:590`), while the measured cost was on
`build_flat_variants` → `cargo_build_at(.., None)` (`src/bench.rs:801-806`), and **zero of 1088 arms
use the composed form**, so the fix as stated changes a path nobody runs. The same locus defect exists
in a second config key (`config.rs:140-147`, read only at `src/bench_gen.rs:78`).

**One axis converted "unpriced" into a structural number without guessing at seconds.** Censused over
all 1088 arm crates:

```
mockspace-bench-harness : 0     <- the load-bearing result
mockspace-bench-core    : 1088
mockspace-bench-macro   : 1073
mockspace-bench-matrix  : 594
```

**No arm links the orchestrator.** That is the boundary claim worth keeping, and it is what the
four-crate split buys: the 12,781-line harness crate stays out of every measured artifact. A stronger
sentence in one source, that an arm links `bench-core` and `bench-macro` "and nothing else", is
**false** and is contradicted by the two files it cites; the coordinator re-ran the census
independently rather than carrying it.

Not one arm varies the framework's feature axis, and the only partition is the pin, of which there are
three in the corpus. So the framework crates and their proc-macro dependencies compile 1088 times
where three to six would do, and that sharing is immune to the feature-unification hazard the earlier
probe found, which bounds only the consumers' own support crates.

**The wall-clock question remains unpriced and both said so.** The current harness cannot host it: it
is a per-call runtime harness with calibration, batches and dlopen, and a build-time comparison has no
per-call quantity. The measurement is specified in the second source, including parallelism as an axis
rather than held at one.

## The finding that came out of building that probe

**The eight-combination matrix passes on the broken tree.** It varies one knob at a time, so the member
always declares the knob being read and a level that resets the other four is invisible. Only a case
declaring one knob and asserting the others fires. A reviewer looking at a complete-looking matrix
would reasonably call the surface covered.

That case now exists as a test for both member forms **on the collection branch**
(`feat/bench-hygiene-collection`, PR #28, not yet on `dev`), each with a negative control reproducing
the defect's signature. Before that it existed only inside probe 08.

## Also found, and not yet placed in this round

`report::generate` indexes an empty variant list at `report.rs:17`, with the guards at `:36` and `:42`

## Answered by op: a non-resolving role refuses at load

**Settled.** A bench declaring a `floor` or `baseline` that names an arm which does not resolve is
**refused at load**. No results, no history row, non-zero exit. The refusal names the unresolved role
and lists the arms that do exist.

The options op chose among were:

1. **Refuse at load.** Nothing runs and nothing is written. A typo blocks the run rather than
   degrading it.
2. **Run and record `resolved: false`**, with the report omitting the normalisation and `CellRecord`
   carrying that the role did not resolve, so a wrong number exists on disk but is correctly
   labelled.
3. **Refuse only when the run would publish**, degrading a local run and refusing one that appends to
   history or writes a report.

He took the first. It matches the expert weight, which was on refusing, unhedged.

**What this settles beyond the one key.** The question was whether a bench whose normalisation did not
resolve should produce artifacts at all, and the answer is no. That generalises to the three declared
roles together rather than to `floor` alone, and it makes the preflight resolution of `bench_name` a
prerequisite rather than an option, since the refusal has to know which arms exist before it can list
them.

### What implementing it taught, which the sitting had not found

The refusal landed in `BenchManifest::validate_roles` against the **declared** arm spellings, which is
the part reachable without loading an arm. Three corrections came out of the test suite rather than
out of review, and each was the first attempt being wrong:

**`delta` is not a role naming an arm.** It names a normalisation mode (`subtract`, `ratio`,
`percent`, `none`). The sitting's phrase "the three declared analysis roles" is accurate about how the
keys are grouped in the manifest and misleading about what they refer to: only `baseline` and `floor`
name arms. A refusal checking `delta` against the arm list rejects every correct manifest that sets
one.

**An arm's name is not its declared spelling.** A path entry ending `libnative.dylib` carries an arm
named `native`, because the platform dylib prefix is not part of the name. A check comparing stems
alone rejects correct manifests, and an error message built from stems tells someone who wrote
`kernel` that the available arms are `libkernel`. Both were caught by existing tests.

**A sweep that narrows its arms while inheriting a baseline is a live hazard, and it was sitting in
the test corpus as a fixture.** `arms = ["kernel", "native"]` with `baseline = "native"` at the bench
level, and a sweep keeping only `kernel`, inherits a baseline that resolves to nothing. The sweep's
report then renders as though no baseline were declared, which is exactly the silence op refused.

That last one is the interesting one, because **role inheritance and arm narrowing compose into a
third state neither key describes on its own**. The refusal now covers it and the fixture declares its
own baseline. Whether narrowing should instead *drop* an inherited role that no longer applies is a
design question this round has not asked, and refusing is the conservative answer until it does.
