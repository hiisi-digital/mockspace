# Topic: where the bench configuration lives

**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `bench-matrix/`, `bench-harness/src/matrix.rs`, `bench-harness/src/config.rs`, `src/bench.rs`, and the consumer trees
**Source:** two expert investigations on `docs/sweep-investigation` and `docs/sweep-consumer-view`, correcting a measurement in the design on `docs/bench-ergonomics-survey`

A redesign proposed keeping sweep configuration inline, on the measurement that of roughly 240
future sweeps only two carry anything beyond a point list and an arm list. **That measurement was
taken on a generated file.** The premise for inline is dead; what replaces it is open.

## The correction

`bench.toml` is generator output for the largest consumer. `generate_all` rewrites everything after
a marker (`bench-matrix/src/generate.rs:41,102-134`), and in vehje that is **3888 of 4956 manifest
lines, 135 of 180 bench sections, and 594 of 902 arm crates**. Counting keys there measures the
generator's rendering rather than anyone's input.

What a person writes is a `bench_matrix!` block in Rust
(`vehje/mock/benches/carrier/src/bench/cfg.rs:31-40`), and the sweep already exists there with ten
declarative keys: name, crate path, crate dep, extra deps, seed, the axis and its values, sizes,
baseline, regime, setup, plus per-cell feature gates and per-cell setup overrides.

**Five of those categories have no `bench.toml` expression at all**: the sweep axis, the regime, the
setup entry point, per-arm cargo features, and the variant dep lines.

## The same wall, hit twice, solved differently

`#[bench_variant]` permits exactly one const generic parameter (`bench-macro/src/lib.rs:55-56`), so
arvo packs two to four sweep fields into the single integer point:
`KEY = W*10000 + NC*1000 + OP*100 + D` (`warm-container-shared/src/lib.rs:59`) and four siblings.
**36 of arvo's 49 sections carry a packed key, holding 87 per-sweep constant settings and 40
varying axes inside an integer.**

The framework's own note predates both: "**multi-sweep.** Either add cartesian multi-sweep, or keep
it a single flattened sweep with composite values" (`bench-matrix/TODO.md:60-63`). arvo chose the
second, in a different repo, without the note. vehje's `carrier_coldcycle` is an entire forked
family whose only difference from `carrier_predecode` is `regime: cold_cycle(16)`, with a doc
comment telling the reader to join the two by hand.

## Matrix and sweep are two things, and they nest

Correcting a recollection that they were one renamed to the other: harness `MatrixSpec`/`AxisSpec`
landed 2026-07-22 (`1315109`), `bench-matrix` `SweepAxis`/`CellDecl` on 2026-07-23 (`2471643`).

**A cell is one arm, one cdylib.** **A sweep is the outer axis producing one bench per value.**
`bench_matrix` uses exactly one axis of the harness's N-axis machinery (`generate.rs:95`) and hand
loops the sweep outside it.

This also refutes a claim in the proposed design, which defines "cell" as one *(bench, sweep,
point)* and states the word is already used for that in `bench-matrix`. It is not, and under the
design's own vocabulary the object it describes is an **arm**.

## The duplication the layout question was really about

The same facts are written three times. For `bitpack-carrier-width`, six integers appear
**thirteen times**: six arm attributes, one manifest entry, six driver match arms. Across arvo the
driver's table is 256 live match arms against 256 size rows, and 47 sections against 47 distinct
section-and-type pairs, which means the routine type is a function of the section name alone and the
point selects nothing.

So the 672-line driver is roughly 250 lines the library already provides (kirjo does the same job in
60 on the same branch) plus a 258-line table that is 47 facts and 209 lines of restatement.

## What this topic must settle

- **What a sweep carries.** Five categories are observed in Rust today with no config expression.
  Whether they move, and if so where, is the question. A sweep that could move the seed table, the
  anti-hoist, the S/I split, the digest, the calibration floor or the build profile would make two
  sweeps of one bench incomparable, so those stay the harness's whatever else moves.
- **Whether multi-axis sweeps are expressed or kept flattened.** Both consumers independently
  flattened, one by packing an integer and one by forking a family. That is two votes from practice
  and neither had the choice.
- **What the consumer writes and what the tool generates.** The typed dispatch table cannot be a
  `macro_rules!` iteration over a const list, because monomorphisation needs literal tokens; that is
  an argument for generating it rather than for a macro, and it decides whether a consumer driver
  exists at all.
- **The vocabulary**, now that cell and arm are known to collide, and that the per-file bench form
  has been settled as having no wrapper table.
- **The benchspace membership**, which is settled in shape (`[benchspace] members`, default `["**"]`,
  declared rather than detected) and needs its schema written now that the sweep answer may change
  what a member contains.

## What is not in scope here

The validation defects, which have their own topic file and come first. The metadata record, which
has its own and which this topic must not contradict: whatever a sweep turns out to carry becomes
part of a cell's recorded identity.
